What it is

What is GPT-6 Astra?

GPT-6 Astra is OpenAI's newest and most capable model. It was released on September 3, 2026.

The big new thing is computer use. Astra can look at your screen, move the mouse, type, and click through apps and websites to finish a task. You tell it what you want done, and it does it.

👉🏽 OpenAI describes it this way: "Anything you can do on a computer, Astra can do for you."
What it can do

What it can do for you.

Work inside the apps you already use

It uses your apps the way you would, through the screen. That includes tools that don't connect to AI at all.

Example: updating a customer record, filling out an online form, organizing your calendar.

Finish work that takes several steps

It can move between apps and keep going until the whole task is done.

Example: building a document, spreadsheet, or presentation from start to finish.

Keep working while you step away

Longer tasks can keep running without you watching every click.

Work faster than before

OpenAI says it's about twice as fast at computer use as its last model.

How to turn it on

How to turn it on.

Astra is included in ChatGPT Plus, Pro, Business, and Enterprise. It uses your existing plan, so there's no extra charge.

👉🏽 You might open ChatGPT and say, "I don't see it." If you don't, you're not doing anything wrong. On the Plus plan, Astra isn't in regular Chat. It's in Work and Codex.
  • Open the ChatGPT desktop app and update it to the latest version.
  • In the top-left menu, switch from Chat to Work.
  • To let it use your computer, go to Plugins → Computer Use and turn it on.
  • On a Mac, allow Screen Recording and Accessibility when it asks.
  • Start a task by typing @Computer and saying what you want done.
Three real examples

Three real examples.

These come from people who tested Astra and wrote up what happened.

1 · It set up a business workflow on its own

A reviewer on the How I AI show asked Astra to update their lead system, so every new lead would go to the right business partner and get a custom email. Astra took over their Chrome browser and built the whole thing. Doing it by hand had taken them about an hour.

What this means for you: it can work inside the tools you already pay for.

2 · It made a YouTube thumbnail across two apps

The same reviewer had Astra make a thumbnail. It created the images in one design tool, then opened Figma and put together the finished thumbnail, all while their designer was out.

What this means for you: it can take on the repetitive part of creative work, and you still make the final call.

3 · It built financial models four times faster than the champion

OpenAI reports that Astra finished Financial Modeling World Cup challenges about four times faster than the winning human competitor. This is OpenAI's own result, not an independent test.

What this means for you: less time building the spreadsheet, more time deciding what the numbers mean.

Sources: the How I AI review of GPT-6 Astra (ChatPRD) and OpenAI's announcement.

Be careful

What to be careful with.

  • Don't give it your passwords. Sign in yourself, then let it work inside that session.
  • Tell it what not to do. It won't guess, so say it plainly in your instructions.
  • Start with something low-stakes. Don't point it at live business data you haven't backed up.
  • Some tasks are blocked. OpenAI rates Astra's cybersecurity ability at its highest risk level, so hacking-related tasks are turned off on regular plans.
My real example

What I built with Codex.

I had a PDF, and I wanted people to see part of it, then give their email to open the rest.

I asked Codex to review the workflow. My prompt: "Please look at this and see if this could be accomplished here," followed by my gated-guide tutorial link. Codex reviewed it and confirmed it could build the webpage and email gate.

  • I gave Codex the document's location. I shared the path to my CHES study guide PDF on my desktop. Codex read it and turned it into a mobile-friendly webpage, preserving all ten topics and 31 knowledge checks.
  • Codex built the email gate and published the page. The first topic is freely readable. After someone enters an email, the remaining nine topics load from the server. Codex deployed the page to my Vercel account and tested the unlock.
  • We attempted to connect email collection. I supplied a Systeme.io key, and Codex saved it as a secret in Vercel. Testing showed it was an MCP key rather than the required Public API key. The webpage and unlock work; saving emails to Systeme.io remains unfinished.
