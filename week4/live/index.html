Recap

Pricing that converts.

Quick refresher from last week, how you show the price matters as much as the price itself:

  • Three tiers, highlight the middle one as "Most Popular." People anchor to the middle.
  • One clear, bold price per tier. Never make them hunt for it.
  • Lead with monthly, add annual (with a discount) once you have traction.
  • Short benefit list, three or four lines, not a feature dump.
  • One consistent button on every tier, and reduce the risk with a trial.
This week

Always tell the user what's happening.

This is the #1 usability rule from the Nielsen Group: visibility of system status. The app should always keep people informed about what's going on, with clear feedback, quickly.

A frozen screen doesn't say "loading." It says "broken."

It splits into two jobs, and both matter double for AI apps, because AI takes a few seconds to respond:

Loading states

Show that the app is working while it thinks, so no one stares at a blank screen wondering if it froze.

Error messages

When something breaks, say what happened in plain language and what to do next, never a scary code or a dead end.

The words to know

Say them out loud so they stop being scary.

Pivotal = build this in. Basics = the simple staples. Polish = nice later, skip for now.

Streaming Pivotal

The response appears word-by-word as it's generated, instead of making them wait for the whole thing.

You've seen it: Claude and ChatGPT "typing" the answer out live.

Typing / "thinking" indicator Pivotal

The little dots or "thinking…" shown while the AI or an agent is working, so it doesn't feel frozen.

You've seen it: the three bouncing dots in any chat app.

Graceful fallback / retry Pivotal

When something fails, show a plain message and a way to try again, instead of crashing or going blank.

You've seen it: "That took too long, tap to try again." AI calls fail more (rate limits, timeouts), so this is a must.

Inline validation Pivotal

The error shows right at the field as they type, not after they submit.

You've seen it: typing an email with no "@" and getting "Please enter a valid email" under the box instantly. Matters for your login and sign-up forms.

Spinner Basics

The little spinning icon that means "working on it."

You've seen it: the circle that spins while a page loads.

Progress bar Basics

Shows how far along a task is, best when you know roughly how long it'll take.

You've seen it: an upload or download filling up from 0 to 100%.

Skeleton screen Polish

Gray placeholder shapes of the content while it loads. Feels faster than a spinner.

You've seen it: the gray boxes on Facebook or YouTube before posts appear.

Optimistic UI Polish

Show the result instantly (assume it worked), and quietly undo it if it fails. Feels instant.

You've seen it: the Instagram heart turning red the moment you tap, before the server confirms.

Toast Polish

A small message that pops up briefly to confirm an action or an error, then disappears.

You've seen it: a "Saved!" or "Copied to clipboard" bar sliding up for a couple seconds.
👉 The four Pivotal ones (streaming, thinking indicator, graceful fallback, inline validation) are what actually make an AI chat, voice, or agent app feel trustworthy. Nail those first, the rest is polish.

New this week

AI disclosure: quick and clean.

As of August 2, the EU's AI transparency rules are enforceable, and they reach the US. The law follows the user, so if someone in the EU can reach your bot, you're in scope. Several US states and the FTC already require it too.

Three rules apply to you. That's really it.
  • Your bot says it's "AI" in its first message. The word "AI," not "virtual assistant," plus a visible label in the chat.
  • AI video or audio of a realistic-looking person gets a label up front. Dragons and fantasy don't count; a realistic AI avatar does.
  • Published AI text that informs people gets a label, or a named human's name on it.
👉 The part people get wrong: the "a human reviewed it" exception only covers published text. It does not excuse your chatbot from disclosing, and it doesn't make a deepfake label optional. Bots and AI media disclose no matter what.
Nobody left because a bot said "I'm an AI." They leave when they figure it out and feel played.
