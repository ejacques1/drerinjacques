What's happening

OpenAI is retiring custom GPTs.

This is straight from OpenAI's own help page. They're retiring custom GPTs and moving creators to something called plugins.

  • Dec 11, 2026Custom GPTs retire. OpenAI's wording: custom GPTs and their GPT pages "become inaccessible."
  • Feb 11, 2027A later date, but only for FedRAMP workspaces and Enterprise workspaces with an approved deferral. This almost certainly isn't you.
👉🏽 Your old chats are safe. OpenAI says existing conversations with custom GPTs stay accessible after retirement. It's the GPT itself that stops working.

It affects all ChatGPT plans. And if you use someone else's GPT, that one is going away too.

The replacement

What's a plugin?

A plugin is OpenAI's replacement for a GPT. It bundles your instructions, your reference files, and connected apps together.

If you created a GPT, you go to My GPTs and select Migrate to plugin.

What carries over

Your instructions become a "skill" inside the plugin. Your knowledge files get copied over as reference files. Connected apps come along.

What does not

Custom actions don't transfer. If your GPT connects to an outside service, that breaks, and rebuilding it may take a developer. The model you picked doesn't carry over either.

⚠️ Migrating makes the original read-only, and you can't delete it. Finish any edits you care about before you migrate.

Here's the catch.

If you built your GPT to share it, read this part twice.

  • A migrated plugin starts private. OpenAI's words: migration "does not automatically install it or give other people access."
  • The people using your GPT today don't come with it. They lose access and don't automatically get the replacement.
  • Making a personal plugin public requires a separate submission process. It's not a switch you flip.
  • Prefilling your old audience is a managed-workspace feature. On a personal account you don't get it.
👉🏽 So if you were using a GPT as something you give out, promote, or sell access to, the replacement doesn't do that job out of the box. That's the real story this week.
Do this today

Get your instructions out. Right now.

Before anything else, go save what you wrote. Those instructions are the actual work, and they're sitting on a platform with a shutdown date.

  • Open ChatGPT and go to My GPTs.
  • Open your GPT and click Edit (or Configure).
  • Select all the text in the Instructions box and copy it.
  • Paste it into a document on your own computer and save it.
  • Download any knowledge files you uploaded, so you have your own copies.
👉🏽 Do this even if you plan to migrate. Takes two minutes, and it's the thing you can't get back.
The real fix

Own it instead.

A custom GPT was always a rental. It lives inside ChatGPT, your people need a ChatGPT account to use it, you can't charge for it, and OpenAI can retire it. Which is exactly what just happened.

The alternative is to take the same instructions and rebuild them as your own web app: your link, your login, your subscription, your customers.

Easier now

You don't need VS Code anymore.

When I first taught this, you had to install VS Code and work inside it. A lot of you hated that part, and honestly, it was the step where most people quit.

You can skip it. Claude Code and Codex both work in their own app now. And instead of pasting keys around, you connect your tools once and they stay connected.

Do this first, before you build anything

In Claude or in ChatGPT, go to Settings → Connectors and connect the tools you'll use.

GitHub · Supabase · Stripe · Vercel

Once those are connected, you can just say what you want and it can act in those tools for you, instead of you clicking through four dashboards and copying keys between them.

👉🏽 This is the biggest quality-of-life change since I first taught this. If you started the tutorial and gave up at the VS Code part, start again from here.
Then get users

Five places to post your app.

We went deep on this last week. Here's the short version, because it's what you do the moment the app is live: don't post it everywhere, post it to these five first.

  • There's An AI For That. People go there to find an AI tool for a specific job, so they're already looking for something like yours.
  • AlternativeTo. List your app as an alternative to a big-name tool, and you'll reach people who are already looking to switch.
  • Capterra. If your app is for businesses, this is where small business owners compare software before they buy.
  • The subreddit where your customers hang out. Not the builder subreddits, the ones for whoever your app is for. Don't just drop a link. Say you built it, explain the problem it solves, and ask for feedback.
  • ChatifyIT as your link in bio. The one everyone skips, and the most important. You give it one sentence and its AI builds a free offer right on your link-in-bio page. Every visitor who grabs it leaves their email, so you're building a list of people who want what you offer.
