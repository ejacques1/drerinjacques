Google Fonts Can Get Your Vibe-Coded Site Sued in Europe. Here’s the One-Prompt Fix

Those fonts on your site are probably loading from Google, and every visit sends your visitor’s IP address along with them. A German court has already called that illegal. Here’s how to fix it in one prompt.

By Dr. Erin JacquesHuman ideas, AI assist. Upfront, as always.

November 8, 2026

Vibe coding is now one of the easiest ways to get sued in 2026, and the reason might be your fonts.

You’re probably thinking, “I would never hand my visitors’ personal data to another company without asking.” But if your site loads its fonts from Google, you already are.

It happens on every single visit, before anyone clicks anything. And in Europe, a court has already ruled that it breaks the law.

How do Google Fonts send your visitors’ data to Google?

The fonts usually aren’t stored on your site. Your page tells each visitor’s browser to go fetch them from Google’s servers, and that request carries the visitor’s IP address to Google.

Those beautiful fonts AI picks for your site are very often Google Fonts. The quickest way to add them is a line of code that points to fonts.googleapis.com. It works, it looks great, and it quietly connects every visitor to Google the moment your page loads. No cookie banner stops it, because it happens before the banner even appears.

Is it really illegal to use Google Fonts?

Using the fonts isn’t the problem. Loading them from Google’s servers without the visitor’s consent is, at least under Europe’s privacy law, the GDPR.

In January 2022, a regional court in Munich ruled that a website passed a visitor’s IP address to Google without permission just by embedding Google-hosted fonts. The court said an IP address counts as personal data, because it’s possible to trace it back to a person, whether or not anyone actually does. The site owner had to pay the visitor €100 in damages (case 3 O 17493/20) and stop sending IP addresses to Google.

The bigger number was the warning. If the site kept doing it, the court threatened a penalty of up to €250,000 per violation. After the ruling, German website owners started receiving a wave of warning letters over Google Fonts, many asking for money.

What do people get wrong about this?

“The fine was €250,000.” It wasn’t. The actual award was €100. The €250,000 is the maximum the court threatened for anyone who kept doing it after being ordered to stop. Small number today, big number if you ignore it.

“I’m in the US, so this doesn’t apply to me.” Not necessarily. The GDPR can apply to businesses outside Europe that offer goods or services to people in Europe. The US doesn’t have an equivalent font ruling, so don’t let anyone tell you it’s a US law.

“Every vibe-coded site has this problem.” Not every one. Some tools already handle it. Next.js, for example, has a built-in font tool that downloads Google Fonts when your site is built and serves them from your own site, so no requests are sent to Google by the visitor’s browser. But you can’t assume which way your AI wired it. You have to check.

Your visitors never agreed to meet Google. Your fonts introduced them anyway.

What’s changing: AI makes the choice for you

Fonts used to be a decision a developer made on purpose, and a careful one would host them on the site. Now AI picks them for you in seconds and adds them the quickest way, which is often a link straight to Google.

That means more people are launching sites faster, with privacy details nobody looked at. The good news is the fix got just as fast. The same AI that wired your fonts to Google can move them onto your own site in a few minutes.

How do you fix it? Your move

  1. Host your fonts on your own site. Paste this into the AI you build with:

The prompt

Find every font in my app that loads from Google's servers or any outside server. Download those font files, host them on my own site, and update my code to load them locally. Then scan the whole site and show me anything still calling Google or any third party for fonts, so no visitor's IP address leaves my site.
  1. Check that it worked. Open your live site, right-click, choose Inspect, and open the Network tab. Reload the page and type fonts.g in the filter box. You should see no requests to fonts.googleapis.com or fonts.gstatic.com.
  2. Look for other outside requests. Ask your AI: “List every outside service my site loads when a page opens, before the visitor clicks anything.” Fonts are the most common one, but embedded maps, videos and scripts follow the same logic. This step is my recommendation, not part of the ruling.

This one keeps you safe and puts you ahead: your site looks exactly the same, and your visitors’ data stays on your site. I’m a professor, not a lawyer, so if you serve a lot of European customers, talk to one about the rest of your setup.

Keep the fonts. Just stop sending your visitors to Google to get them.
