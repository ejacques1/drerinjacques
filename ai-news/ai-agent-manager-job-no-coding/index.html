AI Agent Manager: The New AI Job That Doesn’t Require Coding

Companies are putting AI agents to work, and they need people to manage them. The listings ask for expertise and judgment, not code.

By Dr. Erin JacquesHuman ideas, AI assist. Upfront, as always.

October 30, 2026

There’s a new job showing up at major companies, and some versions of it pay over $200,000.

You don’t need to know how to code. You don’t need a tech degree.

It’s called an agent manager. You’ll also see it listed as agent ops, AI agentic operations, or AI operations manager.

What is an AI agent manager?

An agent manager is the person in charge of the AI agents a company puts to work. You track what the agents get right and what they get wrong, and you teach them new skills.

Harvard Business Review introduced the role in February 2026. Suraj Srinivasan and Vivienne Wei describe agent managers as leaders “responsible for orchestrating how AI agents learn, collaborate, perform, and work safely alongside humans,” according to the article. They compare it to the product manager, a job that barely existed before the software boom and is now everywhere.

That’s the pattern. A new kind of work shows up, and someone has to own it.

Does an AI agent manager job require coding?

Most listings don’t ask for it. Here’s what they actually ask for: program management or product management, service delivery, real expertise in a field like finance, healthcare, customer support or operations, and comfort using AI tools.

Take Anthropic’s AI Outcomes Manager role. The person helps large companies adopt Claude. The listing asks for 6+ years in customer success, account management or consulting, plus “technical fluency” with AI. It doesn’t ask you to be an engineer. It does prefer people who’ve built with Claude Code, so hands-on time with AI tools counts.

Earlier this year, a Databricks AI operations listing went further: the role owned AI agent rollouts, training and performance tracking, and the technical build was someone else’s job.

How much does an AI agent manager make?

It depends on the company and the level, and the range is wide. Anthropic’s AI Outcomes Manager role lists $265,000 to $320,000 base. The Databricks AI operations program manager role paid $76,000 to $106,000.

So $200K+ is real, but it’s the senior end. Those roles go to people with years of experience running programs or accounts, plus deep knowledge of one industry.

That’s the part people miss. The money follows the expertise, not the job title.

What’s changing?

Managing used to mean managing people. Now part of managing is managing the AI agents doing the work.

Companies are moving agents from tests into real operations: answering customers, processing requests, writing first drafts. Every one of those agents needs someone who knows what good work looks like in that field.

AI can do the task. It can’t tell when the answer is wrong for your customer. You can.

AI can do the task. You’re the one who knows when it’s wrong.

How do I become an AI agent manager?

This is my own approach, built from what these listings ask for.

  1. Read three listings. Search LinkedIn Jobs for “AI operations manager,” “agent ops” and “AI agent manager.” Highlight every requirement you already meet.
  2. Manage one AI assistant for two weeks. Pick a real task from your work, like drafting client follow-ups, sorting requests or summarizing reports. Set it up in Claude or ChatGPT.
  3. Keep a log. Write down what it got right, what it got wrong, and the instruction you added to fix it. That log is the job.
  4. Turn the log into one résumé line. For example: “Set up and managed an AI assistant for client follow-ups; cut drafting time from 3 hours a week to 1.” Use your own numbers.

You’ll walk into the interview with something most candidates don’t have: proof you’ve already managed AI.

This helps you stand out.

The people who win this shift won’t be the ones who learned to code. They’ll be the ones who learned to manage what the code does.
