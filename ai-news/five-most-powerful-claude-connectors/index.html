The 5 Most Powerful Claude Connectors for Anyone Building an App

These five connectors let Claude test your app, lock down your database, find where visitors leave, size up your competitors and announce what you ship. Here’s what each one does and how to connect it.

By Dr. Erin JacquesHuman ideas, AI assist. Upfront, as always.

November 3, 2026

Claude can build your app. On its own, it can’t see how your app is doing.

It can’t click through it like a customer. It can’t check who can read your database. It doesn’t know where visitors give up, what your competitors charge or who knows you just shipped something new.

Connectors fix that. Here are the five most powerful ones, and the fifth is the one almost everyone skips.

What is a Claude connector?

A connector plugs Claude into another tool so it can use that tool for you. Under the hood, most connectors use the Model Context Protocol (MCP), an open standard for linking AI to apps. In the Claude app you add them under Customize, then Connectors. In Claude Code you add them with one command.

1. Playwright: test your app like a real customer

Playwright lets Claude open your app in a real browser and click through it the way a customer would: sign up, log in, check out. It finds what’s broken before your customers do.

Playwright runs in Claude Code, not the regular Claude chat app. In your project folder, run:

claude mcp add playwright npx '@playwright/mcp@latest'

That’s the command from Simon Willison’s Playwright setup guide. Then try: “Open my app, sign up as a new user and tell me everything that breaks.”

2. Supabase: find the database holes strangers can see

If AI built your app on Supabase, this one matters most for safety. Claude can run Supabase’s security check and flag problems like tables any stranger can read, then fix them for you.

Supabase is now an official Claude connector. Supabase’s own setup guide recommends connecting to a development project first, using read-only mode on anything with real customer data, and approving each action before it runs. Then try: “Run the security advisors on my project and explain each problem in plain English.”

Claude can build your app. Connectors let it see how your app is actually doing.

3. PostHog: see exactly where visitors leave

PostHog is product analytics. Connected to Claude, it lets Claude query your data and tell you where visitors drop off before they sign up, so you know what to fix first.

In Claude Code, PostHog’s docs give this command, then you log in the first time you use it:

claude mcp add --transport http posthog https://mcp.posthog.com/mcp -s user

Then try: “Where do most visitors leave before signing up? What should I fix first?” You’ll need PostHog tracking on your site for it to have data to read.

4. Firecrawl: read your competitors’ pages

Firecrawl lets Claude search the web and read pages as clean text. Point it at a competitor and Claude can lay out what they offer and what they charge.

Firecrawl’s MCP server connects at https://mcp.firecrawl.dev/v2/mcp. Search and basic reading work without a key, with limits; a free Firecrawl account unlocks more. Then try: “Read the pricing pages of my top three competitors and compare them to mine in a table.” Use it for research, not to copy anyone’s site.

5. ChatifyIT: tell people what you shipped

This is the most important one, and it’s the one everyone skips. Full disclosure: I built it.

Every time you ship something, Claude writes the LinkedIn post and the email about it and saves them to your ChatifyIT dashboard. You approve it, and it goes to your LinkedIn and your email list. Nothing goes out until you click.

In Claude, go to Customize, then Connectors, then Discover, and search “ChatifyIT.” In Claude Code, add it with https://www.chatifyit.com/api/mcp. The connector is on ChatifyIT’s Growth and Pro plans.

Why is the fifth one the most important?

Because the first four make your app better, and none of that matters if nobody knows it exists. A fixed bug, a safer database and a better signup page only pay off when people come back to see them.

What’s changing: one person can now run a whole product team

Testing, security, analytics, research and marketing used to be five different jobs. With connectors, Claude can do the first pass on all five while you make the decisions.

The builders who get ahead aren’t the ones with the most tools. They’re the ones who connect the right ones and check the work.

Your move

  1. Pick the connector for your biggest problem today. Broken app? Playwright. Worried about data? Supabase. No signups? PostHog.
  2. Connect it and run the “then try” prompt above. Read what Claude finds before you let it change anything.
  3. Add ChatifyIT so every fix you ship becomes a post and an email.

This one gives you leverage: one person, five jobs covered, and you stay in charge of every change.

Your app is only as good as what you can see. Connect one tonight.
