2 · Your list

Start with people who already know you.

Your first customers are already in your phone and inbox. Export them once, bring them into Systeme.io, and you have a list you own, no algorithm in the way.

Export your contacts

Gmail

Google Contacts

Go to contacts.google.com → select all → Export → choose Google CSV.

Outlook

Outlook / Microsoft

Open People → Manage contacts → Export contacts → save as CSV.

iPhone / Mac

iCloud

icloud.com/contacts → select all → Export vCard. Then import that file into Google Contacts and export it as CSV.

Bring them into Systeme.io

  1. No account yet? Create your Systeme.io account first.
  2. In Systeme.io, open Contacts and choose Import.
  3. Upload your CSV and match the columns: email, first name, last name.
  4. Add a tag like my-network so you can email just this group.
  5. Clean it up: remove anyone you'd never actually email (old vendors, no-reply addresses).
👉 These are people who know you, so warm them up gently. Start with a small batch and grow from there. The full import and warm-up rules are in Week 4, Video 2.
3 · Your first 3 emails

The Soap Opera Sequence.

Russell Brunson borrowed this from the soap operas we grew up on: every episode ends on a cliffhanger, so you have to tune in tomorrow. Your emails work the same way. Each one tells part of your story and ends with a hook that makes them watch for the next one.

Email 1 · Day 1

Set the stage

Who you are, why you're writing, and a promise: "Over the next few days I'm going to tell you how I ended up building ___." End on an open loop.

Email 2 · Day 2

The backstory

Where you were stuck (the wall), how it felt, and the moment everything clicked (your epiphany). End by teasing what you built.

Email 3 · Day 3

The reveal

What you built, who it's for, the benefits they didn't expect, and a clear invitation to check out your platform.

Every email ends on a cliffhanger. That's what gets tomorrow's email opened.

Let AI interview you and write them

Paste this into Claude or ChatGPT. It asks you the questions Brunson uses to find your story, one at a time, then writes all three emails in your voice, promoting your platform.

4 · Create your LinkedIn Page

Give your platform its own home on LinkedIn.

Your personal profile is you. A LinkedIn Page is your platform, so people can follow it, and it shows up when they search for tools like yours. Takes about 10 minutes.

  1. On LinkedIn, click For Business (the grid icon, top right) → Create a Company Page.
  2. Choose Company.
  3. Add your platform name, pick your LinkedIn public URL (linkedin.com/company/yourname), and add your website.
  4. Choose your industry, company size (2–10 is fine), and type.
  5. Upload your logo (square, 300×300 or larger) and write a one-line tagline: who it's for + what it does.
  6. Check the verification box and click Create page.
  7. Finish it: add a cover image (1128×191), your About section, and a custom button (like "Visit website" or "Sign up").
  8. On your personal profile, add your platform under Experience so your Page shows up there too.
  9. Click Invite to follow and invite your first connections.
👉 Tip: post from your personal profile for reach, then reshare to your Page. People follow people first.
5 · Post consistently

Show up on a rhythm, not on a whim.

You don't need to go viral. You need to be visible every week, so when someone needs what you built, you're the one they remember.

  • Pick a rhythm you can keep: at least once a week (Pages that post weekly get 2x the engagement). 3 a week beats 10 and then disappearing.
  • Every new feature is a post. "Introducing ___" plus what it does and why it matters.
  • Show the build: behind the scenes, what broke, what you learned.
  • Share wins: a customer result, a milestone, a before and after.
  • Use video when you can, even a 15-second clip.
  • Stay for the first hour: reply to every comment. That's when LinkedIn decides who sees it.
The problem isn't knowing what to post. It's finding the time, every single week.
